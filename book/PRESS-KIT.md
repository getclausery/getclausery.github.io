## Five angles for an editor

1. **The Aquinas angle.** A thirteenth-century method applied to a twenty-first-century feed. The comparison
   is stated with its four limits rather than as a slogan — which is itself the story.
2. **The genealogy angle.** Manifestation looks perennial. It is datable: Quimby's clinic, Evans's *The
   Mental Cure* (1869), the name "New Thought" in the 1890s, Hopkins, Eddy, the business wing, Neville,
   *The Secret*. Every rule has an author and a year.
3. **The honesty angle.** The book's verdict on the movement it studies is *reject* seven times out of
   fourteen. A religious book that argues against its own audience is unusual enough to report.
4. **The pastoral angle.** Ninety seconds, one page, three colours, one rule about the bedside. This is the
   part pastors actually use, and it is printable.
5. **The falsifiability angle.** Chapter 36 tells the reader how to prove the book wrong: a thirty-day pilot,
   a control group, fruit rather than testimonials. Almost no book in this genre does that.

## Interview questions the book is built to answer

- Why is "your thoughts create reality" attractive to people who have left the Church?
- What does Aquinas's method actually consist of, and where does the Aristotle analogy break down?
- You accept four of the movement's claims. Which four, and why is that the harder position?
- Which of the fourteen verdicts was hardest to write?
- What should a priest say in ninety seconds to someone who asks whether manifesting is a sin?
- Why does the book say it has no authority — and does that not undercut it?
- What would count as evidence that you are wrong?

## What not to say in a pitch

- Not "the Catholic Church teaches" — the book is a research project, not a teaching document, and it says
  so on its copyright page.
- Not "debunks manifestation" — the book opens by granting four of its claims.
- Not "bestselling" or "acclaimed": there are no reviews yet, and a pitch that invents one is dead on
  arrival.
- Not "the only Catholic book on manifestation" — it is the most useful one *for this argument*, which is a
  claim that can be defended.
