# Marketing plan for *The Hidden and the Manifest*

Practical, copy-paste, no paid spend. Written for one person with a free PDF and no platform.

**The product:** a 314-page book, free to read and copy, by Kidus Yared, imprint Clausery, 2026. It is a
theological research project that says so on its copyright page.

**The constraint that decides everything:** the book is free. There is no funnel, no cart, no upsell. So
marketing is not conversion, it is *reach plus credibility* — the only number that matters early is how
many of the right people open it, and the only number that matters later is how many of them quote it to
somebody else.

**The one sentence to sell (use everywhere, verbatim):**

> Manifestation is not a set of false beliefs. It is a set of true observations attached to a false engine.

---

   at that URL, so nothing else can start until it exists.

3. **The email address that answers.** `getclausery@gmail.com` is already the author address in the draft.
   Corrections are the book's whole claim to seriousness, so the page must say where to send them.

## 2. The message architecture

**One sentence** (above). Everything else is a variation.

**Three pitches, by length:**

- **6 words:** *Aquinas meets the Law of Assumption.*
- **25 words:** *A Catholic theology of manifestation: what the movement gets right, what it gets
  catastrophically wrong, and how the Church can baptize the difference.*
- **60 words:** *Manifestation is not a set of false beliefs; it is a set of true observations attached to a
  false engine. This book does with the manifest space what Aquinas did with Aristotle — states it
  honestly, tests each claim, rejects what is false, keeps what is true, and rebuilds the rest inside the
  faith. Free, 314 pages.*

**Ten hooks** (test them; keep the three that move):

1. "Manifestation isn't a false belief. It's a true observation attached to a false engine."
2. "Your thoughts don't create reality. But they do something, and the Church has known what for 800 years."
3. "What Aquinas would do with the Law of Assumption."
4. "The nine-day novena for people who used to journal their manifestations."
5. "Seven rules of manifestation. Every one of them has an author and a date."
6. "Mind cure consolidated in the same decade Thérèse of Lisieux published *Story of a Soul*."
7. "I wrote 314 pages on manifestation and my verdict on most of it is *reject*."
8. "A priest has ninety seconds in a car park. Here is what he should say."
9. "The manifest space won because it has a practice. The Church has seven sacraments and forgot to teach
   anyone how to use them."
10. "This book has no imprimatur, and it says so on page 4."

**The objection you will meet, and the answer:**

> *"This is just Catholic cope."* — "Chapter 13 rejects seven of the fourteen central claims outright, and
> Appendix A lists forty questions I could not answer. If it were cope it would be shorter and kinder."

## 7. Academia.edu, the Internet Archive, and libraries

- **Academia.edu**: upload with the fields in `ACADEMIA.md`. Then add the book to your profile's "Books"
  section so it appears in the sidebar of every paper you upload afterward.
- **Internet Archive**: upload the PDF to `archive.org` with a CC licence. It becomes citable, mirrored,
  and indexed by Google Scholar's crawler far more reliably than a GitHub Pages URL.
- **Zenodo**: mint a DOI (free), which converts "a PDF on a website" into "a publication with an
  identifier". This is the single highest-credibility action in the whole plan.
- **Semantic Scholar / Google Scholar**: after the DOI, submit the record. Citation counts start at zero
  and only move if the DOI exists.

## 8. What to measure (and what to ignore)

Measure, weekly, in a plain text file:

- downloads of the PDF (server logs or Archive/Zenodo counters);
- Academia.edu "views" vs "downloads" — a healthy ratio is roughly 5:1; worse than 20:1 means the abstract
  is overselling or the title is wrong;
- replies to the pastor email (this is the leading indicator, and it is a *quality* signal — three serious
  replies beat three hundred impressions);
- corrections received. The book claims corrigibility in print. Every correction is proof of the claim, so
  count them and publish the ones that change the text.

Ignore: follower counts, likes, and anything that cannot be traced to an open of the PDF.

## 10. The one thing not to do

Do not market it as authoritative, and do not let anyone else market it that way either. The book's
credibility is that it refuses the imprimatur it does not have and asks to be corrected in public. The
moment a post implies "the Church teaches" rather than "a research project argues", the audience that
matters — the pastor, the theologian, the sceptic — stops reading, and the audience that stays is the one
that will be disappointed.
