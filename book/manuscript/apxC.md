---
kind: appendix
title: The Thirty-Day Pilot
lede: A design for a parish or campus test: four weeks, thirty participants, a comparison group, and a commitment to publish the result whether or not it flatters the proposal.
---

**Purpose.** To test whether the transpositions of Part Four produce the fruit the Church's criteria name — prayer, conversion, charity, freedom — in ordinary people, over a month, with a comparison group.

**Participants.** Thirty, recruited openly, with no selection for or against the manifest space. A comparison group of thirty in a second parish receiving equal attention (a weekly meeting and a course on prayer) without the manifest-space content.

**Structure.** Four weeks. One group meeting weekly, forty-five minutes. One daily practice, ten to twenty minutes.

| Week | Practice | Instruction | Group session |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Novena of Desire begins (days 1–7) | one page; a fixed time; the four movements | introductions; the two questions; the relinquishment |
| 2 | Novena concludes (days 8–9), then word and image | written petition; one aspiration; fifteen minutes of *compositio loci* twice | what happened when you asked; the possibility of refusal |
| 3 | Time and body | a fixed morning and night prayer; one bodily practice (kneel, fast, walk) | the body at prayer; what was difficult |
| 4 | Memory, desire, and signs | confession or a conversation with a director; the movements log | writing a personal rule of three practices |

**Measures.** At day 0 and day 30, and again at six months: the ten-item instrument of chapter 36, plus one qualitative interview and one behavioural indicator (acts of charity performed, confessions made, reconciliations).

**What counts as success.** Not enthusiasm. Four movements, in this order of importance:

1. **Safety:** no increase in scrupulosity, self-blame, or blame of the sick; a decrease if possible.
2. **Practice:** a majority praying daily in a set form at day 30, and half still doing so at six months.
3. **Freedom:** participants able to stop for a week without anxiety, and describing God in terms of trust rather than performance.
4. **Charity:** measurable acts of service, and at least one reconciliation per participant where one was needed.

**What counts as failure.** Any of the following: participants become dependent on the group leader; the practice is described as a technique; scrupulosity rises; the six-month follow-up shows collapse; the qualitative interviews describe a God who must be managed. If the pilot fails, publish it and redesign.

**Ethics.** Written consent; anonymity in publication; a named safeguarding contact; referral pathways; no recording without permission; no fee; no promise of any spiritual or physical outcome at any point.

**Cost.** Approximately the cost of printing, refreshments, and one facilitator's time. The pilot is designed to be run by a parish without a budget and without a theologian on staff.
