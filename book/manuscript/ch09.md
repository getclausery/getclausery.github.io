---
title: Creator and Creature
lede: The analogy of being is the single most useful instrument in this book. It is also the least understood. Here is what it does, and why the manifest space cannot survive it.
---

If this book has one technical argument, it is this chapter, and the argument can be stated in a sentence: **the manifest space's errors are almost all instances of one error, which is the refusal of analogy.**

That sounds like the sort of claim a scholastic makes to annoy people. It is not. The refusal of analogy is the most common intellectual mistake of the modern period, and it produces the most characteristic modern disasters, from scientism to pantheism to the belief that the universe is a vending machine with a mood.

::scene

Begin with a puzzle that any child can pose and no one can answer quickly.

God is good. My mother is good. Are they good in the same way?

If yes — if "good" means the same thing in both cases — then either God is good in the way my mother is good (which makes him a creature, a very large and kindly one), or my mother is good in the way God is good (which makes her divine). Both conclusions are absurd, and both have been held by serious people.

If no — if the word means something entirely different in each case — then the sentence "God is good" tells us nothing at all. It is a noise we make in the direction of the mystery. This is the position of a certain kind of apophatic theology and of a certain kind of modern atheism, and it has the same practical result: what we say about God is not true, only pious.

Thomas's answer is neither. The word is used **analogically**: in the same *order* of meaning, but not in the same *mode*. My mother's goodness is a participated goodness, received, limited, and mixed with its opposite. God's goodness is the source of hers, unlimited, and identical with his being. The word is not equivocal (it means something) and not univocal (it does not mean the same thing). It is stretched — and the stretching is not a failure of language but the only honest thing language can do when it speaks of the source of everything.

This is the *analogia entis*, and it does an enormous amount of work. It is what allows the Church to say that creatures are *like* God without being *bits of* God, and that God is *other* than the world without being *unrelated* to it.

::scene

Now apply it to the manifest space, and watch the whole system come apart at one seam.

The manifest space speaks of **energy**. What does the word mean?

In ordinary usage it means one of two things. In physics it means a quantity: the capacity to do work, measured in joules, conserved. In ordinary speech it means vitality: "I don't have the energy today." The manifest space uses the word in a third way, which is neither: a quasi-substantial, quasi-personal, quasi-moral force that is transmitted by thought and feeling, that can be high or low, blocked or flowing, and that attracts like to like.

That third meaning is not an analogy. It is not even an equivocation, because an equivocation is at least aware of the two senses. It is a **univocal concept with a mystical job**: a single thing, imagined on the model of a physical quantity, which is then asked to perform the work that the tradition assigns to grace, providence, love, and the sacraments. The universe has a voltage, and so do you, and the trick is to match them.

The same structure appears in every load-bearing word of the system. **Abundance** is a quantity, so more is better and the spiritual life is a supply curve. **Alignment** is a spatial relation, so the moral life is a matter of getting the pieces to line up. **Vibration** is a physical motion, so the interior life is a frequency. **Block** is an obstruction, so sin is a plumbing problem. **Frequency** is a rate, so prayer is a tuning exercise.

Each of these words is doing something the tradition knows how to do, and doing it by the wrong means. The tradition says: grace. The system says: energy. The tradition says: conformity of will. The system says: alignment. The tradition says: sin. The system says: block. The tradition says: the presence of God. The system says: the frequency.

**A word about quantum.** The system's most respectable-looking argument is that physics has caught up with it: the observer effect, entanglement, the collapse of the wave function, "consciousness creates reality." None of it survives contact with the physics. The "observer" in the observer effect is a measurement interaction, not a mind; entanglement is a correlation, not a channel for intention; the measurement problem is a live dispute about how to interpret the formalism, not a licence to say that your feelings select outcomes. What the appeal accomplishes is a transfer of prestige: technical vocabulary is borrowed to do metaphysical work, and the borrowing is invisible to a reader who has studied neither. That is univocity in its purest form — one word, two domains, no acknowledgement of the gap — and it is worth naming, because a young person who has been told that quantum physics proves manifestation will not be answered by being told that manifestation is unbiblical. She will be answered by someone who can say, in three sentences and without contempt, what the word actually means.

::scene

Why does this matter? Three reasons, and they are practical.

**First, because a univocal concept cannot be worshipped.** You cannot adore a quantity. You can only operate it. This is the deepest pastoral consequence of the error: the manifest space trains people in a relation to reality that has no room for adoration, because adoration is the response to a mystery that exceeds the categories, and there is nothing in the system that exceeds anything. Everything is on the same plane, and so everything is manipulable, and so nothing is holy. The result is a person who has a practice and no reverence, which is a spiritual condition the tradition has a name for: it is the condition of the magician.

**Second, because univocity makes the creature infinite and therefore unbearable.** If the self is a center of the same energy as the universe, then there is no limit that is not a failure. Every boundary becomes a limitation to be overcome, every illness a resistance, every death a defeat of one's frequency. The creature is not allowed to be a creature. And a creature that is not allowed to be a creature will spend its life in a war against its own conditions, which it cannot win, and which it will interpret as a personal defeat.

**Third, because analogy is what makes gratitude possible.** If everything good that happens to me is the return on my own interior investment, then there is nothing to be grateful *for*, only something to be pleased about. Gratitude requires an other who gives. It requires the asymmetry that analogy preserves and univocity destroys. This is why the manifest space's gratitude practice, which is its most attractive feature, is also the place where its metaphysics is most clearly false: a person writing "I am grateful for my new job" nine times a day is not thanking anyone. She is filing a claim.

::scene

Thomas's own use of analogy was not a debating trick. It was the condition of the possibility of theology. If the words we use of God are univocal, we are idolaters; if they are equivocal, we are atheists who make noises. Analogy is the narrow path, and it is narrow for a reason: it requires the speaker to hold in one mind both the likeness and the greater unlikeness, and to keep holding them, and never to let go of either.

The manifest space lets go of both. It takes the likeness (the universe responds! the interior matters!) and drops the unlikeness (the universe is not you, the interior is not the cause). The result is a spirituality of extraordinary intimacy and no reverence — a God you can text, a cosmos that has your number, and a self that is the only thing in the room that is not a projection.

The Catholic answer is not to drop the likeness in reaction. It is to restore the unlikeness, and with it the whole structure that the unlikeness makes possible: creation, providence, grace, sin, redemption, worship, and the possibility of being loved by someone who is not a mirror.

::scene

There is a version of this argument that is easier to feel than the technical one, and it is worth stating because most people will meet the idea before they meet the metaphysics.

Imagine two people praying for the same thing — a job, say — and imagine the two prayers.

The first says: *I am aligning my energy with the outcome. I release resistance. I am open to receiving. I am grateful in advance.* Every sentence is addressed to a mechanism, and every sentence is a technique. If the job comes, the person will feel competent; if it does not, she will feel blocked. In neither case has she spoken to anyone.

The second says: *Lord, I need work. I am afraid. I do not know what you are doing. Help me.* Every sentence is addressed to a person. If the job comes, she will be grateful to someone. If it does not, she will be angry with someone — and anger with someone is a relationship, and a relationship can survive disappointment, and it can even grow in it.

The difference between the two prayers is the *analogia entis*. Not as a doctrine in a book, but as the thing that makes the second prayer possible, because it makes it possible for there to be someone on the other end who is not a mechanism and not a mirror.

That is why this apparently technical chapter sits where it does. The rest of the audit is an application of it.
