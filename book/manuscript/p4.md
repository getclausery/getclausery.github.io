A theology that does not become a practice will be defeated by a practice that has no theology. This part is therefore the most concrete in the book, and the most exposed to being wrong, because practices can be tested and doctrines can only be argued.

It sets out why practice beats doctrine in the formation of human beings; what the grammar of Catholic practice actually is; the transpositions, in four groups — word and image, time and body, memory and desire, signs and guidance; a worked rite, the Novena of Desire, that can be prayed as written; and a triage for the ordinary pastoral case, in which the only question is what to do on Monday.
