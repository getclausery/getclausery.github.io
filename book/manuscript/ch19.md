---
title: Hidden with Christ
lede: The Christian's manifestation is deferred, and the deferral is not a failure. This is the doctrine that makes the whole practice of hope possible — and the one the manifest space cannot survive.
---

There is a sentence in Colossians that ought to be printed on the inside of every manifestation journal in the world, and if it were, the practice would collapse in a week:

> "You have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God. When Christ who is your life is manifested, then you also will be manifested with him in glory." (Col 3:3–4)

Three clauses. **Your life is hidden.** **It is hidden with Christ.** **It will be manifested, later, with him.**

The manifest space is built on the elimination of the gap between the hidden and the manifested. That is its product, its promise, and its entire reason for existing. Colossians says the gap is the structure of the Christian life, and that the attempt to close it is not a technique but a refusal of the cross.

::scene

Begin with the pastoral case, because it is the case that matters and it is the case that nobody writes about.

There is a woman in your parish who has done everything right. She prayed, she went to Mass, she received the sacraments, she raised her children, she cared for her husband through a long illness, she buried him, she kept going. Her life, from the outside, looks like nothing. No promotion, no platform, no visible fruit, no story that would make a good testimony at a conference. If she had been formed by the manifest space, she would have concluded, somewhere in her fifties, that she had failed at the interior life, because the exterior did not match.

Colossians says her life is hidden with Christ in God. That is not a consolation prize. It is a description of where her life actually is. It is stored, kept, held — in the place where Christ is, which is to say, in the safest place there is — and it will be manifested, and the manifestation will be glory, and it will be hers.

The manifest space has no category for this woman. It can only tell her that she did not align. The Church can tell her the truth: that she is not a failure, she is *hidden*, and hiddenness is not the absence of a manifestation; it is its location.

::scene

Now the doctrine, in three steps.

**First: the Christian has died.** Paul is not being poetic. Baptism is a burial; the old self is gone; the life that is now lived is "Christ living in me" (Gal 2:20). This is the most radical claim in the New Testament, and it is the reason the manifest space's anthropology cannot be reconciled with it. The manifest space says: you are an unlimited creative power who has forgotten it. The Gospel says: you are a dead person who has been raised, and the life you have is not yours.

**Second: the life is hidden, with Christ, in God.** The location is specified with a precision that is almost comic. Not "hidden in your potential," not "hidden in your subconscious," not "waiting to be unlocked." Hidden *with Christ*, *in God*. It is a fact about where you are, not a fact about what you have not yet realized, and it is therefore not something a technique can reach or a feeling can confirm.

**Third: the manifestation is future and it is certain.** "When Christ who is your life is manifested, then you also will be manifested with him in glory." The Christian's manifestation is not a possibility to be optimized. It is an event in the future of the world, as certain as the resurrection, and it is not under our administration.

::scene

The contrast with the manifest space's secrecy is worth drawing carefully, because the two look alike and are opposites.

The manifest space instructs people to keep the wish secret — do not tell anyone what you are manifesting, or the energy dissipates, or you will introduce doubt. The secrecy is a technique of protection: the desire is kept in a closed system so that nothing can interfere with it.

The Christian's hiddenness is not a technique and not a secret. It is not that you know something you are not telling; it is that the truth about you is not yet visible, and cannot be made visible by effort. It is hidden *in God* — which means it is known, and known perfectly, by the one in whom it is hidden. The Christian is not keeping a secret from the world. The Christian is being kept.

The practical difference is enormous. The person with a secret is alone with it. The person who is hidden is accompanied.

::scene

There is a whole theology of the hidden life in the tradition, and it is one of the Church's great gifts to a culture of performance.

The thirty years at Nazareth are the foundation of it. The Son of God spent three decades doing nothing that anyone would have written down: working, eating, praying, being a son. The most important life ever lived was, for ninety per cent of its duration, entirely unremarkable. If the manifest space were true, those years were wasted.

Thérèse of Lisieux built an entire spirituality on this — the "little way" — and she did it in a convent at nineteen, with no achievements, no platform, no influence, and a slow death from tuberculosis. Her autobiography is a manual for people whose lives are hidden, and it is one of the most widely read books in the history of the Church, which tells you how many people need it.

And the tradition's word for the virtue that makes hiddenness livable is *humility*, which is not thinking less of yourself but being free from the need to be seen. The manifest space is, at bottom, a technology for being seen — by the universe, by others, by yourself — and it is therefore, at bottom, a technology of pride. Not the crude pride of vanity; the subtle pride of needing your interior life to produce visible results that confirm you exist.

::scene

Now the hard part, because this doctrine can be abused and has been.

The doctrine of hiddenness has been used, by cowards and by institutions, to keep people quiet. "Your suffering is hidden with Christ" has been said to people who needed to be defended, and it has been said by people who wanted them to stop complaining. "Offer it up" is a true sentence and a terrible sentence, depending on who is saying it and to whom.

The distinction is this: hiddenness is a description of *where your life is*, not a prohibition on *speaking about your life*. The one whose life is hidden with Christ is not required to pretend that nothing hurts, and is not required to accept injustice in silence, and is not required to be invisible to the people who ought to see her. Christ himself wept in public, and argued in public, and was executed in public.

What hiddenness rules out is not speech. It is the demand that the world confirm you now. It rules out the project of making your interior life visible on your own terms, on your own schedule, as evidence that you are real. That is the project the manifest space exists to serve, and it is a project that cannot succeed, because the thing it is trying to prove cannot be proved by results.

::scene

One last image, and it comes from the liturgy.

On Holy Saturday, the Church does nothing. The tabernacle is empty, the altar is stripped, the bells are silent, and the faithful are told, in effect, to go home and wait. There is no Mass. The most important thing that has ever happened is happening out of sight, in the dark, in a tomb, and no one is watching, and nothing can be done.

That is the shape of the Christian life for most of its duration. The manifestation is real. It is happening. It is hidden, and it is hidden in God, and it will be shown, and the showing will not be a technique but a resurrection.

The person who can live in Holy Saturday without trying to force Easter is the person who has understood the doctrine. And the Church, which has been keeping Holy Saturday for two thousand years, is the only institution in the world that has any practice for it.
