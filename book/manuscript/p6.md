A book that has argued this hard owes its reader the strongest case against itself.

The objections come from two directions, and they are not symmetric. From the tradition comes the charge that the whole project is a concession dressed as a critique — that to speak of *baptizing* the manifest space is to admit that the Church has lost the argument and is now negotiating terms. From the manifest space comes the charge that the critique is a category error — that it judges a practice by the standards of a metaphysics the practice never claimed, and that the people who practice it have already made their peace with the fact that it is a technique and not a religion.

Both charges are serious. Neither can be answered by a paragraph. The next two chapters take them one at a time, in the voice of the people who make them, and answer as well as the case allows. Where an objection cannot be fully answered, the book says so, because a reader who is told that every question has been settled has been told something false about the subject and something false about books.
