If this book were to leave behind a single artefact, I would want it to be this one: not a treatise, but a body of *quaestiones disputatae* — disputed questions — on the manifest space.

The form is medieval and it is almost absurdly well-suited to the present moment. A question is posed. The strongest objections are stated, in the objector's own voice, and answered on their merits. A contrary authority is cited. Then the master gives his determination, and replies to each objection in turn.

It is a machine for intellectual honesty. It makes the author state the opposing case well enough that the opponent could recognize it. It makes the reader able to check the reasoning at every step. And it produces a short, self-contained unit with a clear question and a clear answer — which is, incidentally, exactly the shape of a good three-minute video.

::scene

### The anatomy

Every question has seven parts, and each part has a job.

**1. The question.** Stated as a yes/no proposition: *Whether the imagination creates reality.* Not "thoughts on manifestation." A determinate question forces a determinate answer.

**2. The objections.** Three or four, in the strongest form, each with an authority or an argument attached. They begin *videtur quod* — "it seems that…" This is the part that most contemporary writing omits, and its omission is the reason so much of it is useless.

**3. The contrary authority.** *Sed contra.* A scriptural verse, a conciliar text, a line from a doctor of the Church. Not a proof; a signpost that the tradition has something to say.

**4. The determination.** *Respondeo dicendum quod.* The master's answer, with the distinctions that make it work. This is where the real work happens, and it is where the reader finds out whether the author has anything to say.

**5. The replies.** One for each objection. Each reply either denies a premise, distinguishes a term, or concedes the point and limits it. A reply that merely restates the determination is not a reply.

**6. The practice.** Not medieval, but necessary for this audience: a short paragraph on what to do differently.

**7. The further questions.** Two or three links to related questions, so that the body of work becomes a network rather than a list.

::scene

### Why the form works now

**It is honest by construction.** You cannot write a good *quaestio* and cheat, because the objections are printed above your answer in the objector's own words, and the reader sees whether you have answered them or merely survived them.

**It is short.** Fifteen hundred words, or three minutes of video. It fits the medium without being deformed by it.

**It is catechetical without being a lecture.** Each question teaches a doctrine by using it. A person who reads forty of them will have learned the whole shape of the faith — creation, grace, providence, sacrament, eschatology — without ever being handed a syllabus.

**It is disputable.** The determination can be argued with. The replies can be contested. This is the opposite of the manifest space's sealed epistemology, and the contrast is itself an argument.

**It is cumulative.** Forty questions, cross-referenced, make a *Summa* in fragments — and fragments are how people actually read now.

::scene

### The forty questions

A proposed body of work. Each would be developed at length; several are foreshortened in this book.

**On God and the universe**
1. Whether the universe can hear.
2. Whether the universe is God.
3. Whether God is an energy.
4. Whether God can be manipulated by technique.
5. Whether God wants you to be rich.
6. Whether God's will can be known from signs.

**On creation and the person**
7. Whether the world is a projection of consciousness.
8. Whether you create your reality.
9. Whether you are God.
10. Whether the self is an illusion.
11. Whether everyone is you pushed out.
12. Whether human beings have a "higher self."
13. Whether the body is a message from the mind.

**On desire**
14. Whether desire is a reliable guide.
15. Whether it is wrong to want things.
16. Whether detachment is a technique.
17. Whether you can want something too much.
18. Whether God gives us the desires of our hearts.

**On thought and speech**
19. Whether the imagination creates reality.
20. Whether thoughts are things.
21. Whether words have power over events.
22. Whether affirmations work.
23. Whether it is wrong to speak of a desired outcome as though it had happened.
24. Whether the name of Jesus can be used as a technique.

**On suffering**
25. Whether illness is attracted by the sufferer.
26. Whether suffering is a lesson.
27. Whether we choose our trials before birth.
28. Whether it is wrong to want to be healed.
29. Whether grief can be manifested away.

**On time and memory**
30. Whether the past can be revised.
31. Whether the future can be known.
32. Whether the dead can be consulted.
33. Whether 11:11 means anything.

**On the Church**
34. Whether a Catholic may practise manifestation.
35. Whether manifestation is a form of divination.
36. Whether a priest should refuse absolution to a manifesting penitent.
37. Whether a Catholic may sell manifestation coaching.
38. Whether the saints can be "manifested."
39. Whether the Church has its own manifestation practices.
40. Whether this whole project is a mistake.

Question 40 is not a joke. Any body of disputed questions that does not include the strongest argument against itself is not a disputation; it is propaganda with footnotes.

### How to build it

**Write the objections first.** Not the answer. The answer will be better if it has been forced to reckon with a well-stated objection, and the objection is the harder half.

**Use the objector's vocabulary.** Not "the New Age teaches that…" but a quotation, with a citation, from a text that a practitioner would recognize as her own.

**Keep the determination short.** Three paragraphs. If it needs ten, the question is really three questions and should be split.

**Publish in public, one at a time, over three years.** One a month. Each one is a video, a page, a podcast segment, a bulletin insert. Each one can be argued with in the comments, which is where the next revision comes from.

**Let others write them.** The body of questions should not be the work of one person; a *Summa* with one author is a system, and a system is exactly what the manifest space is. The form should be open to anyone who will state the objections fairly, and the best answers should be selected by argument, not by platform.

::scene

### The reason this matters more than the book

Books are read once, by a few thousand people, and then they sit. A body of disputed questions is a *toolkit*: it can be excerpted, translated, adapted, argued with, taught from, made into video, put in a bulletin, handed to a catechist, and used by someone who has never heard of this book.

And it has a property that no treatise has: it can be *added to*. Forty questions can become a hundred. A hundred can become the ordinary reference work for a generation of pastors. The manifest space will mutate; the questions can mutate with it, because the form is a method and not a monument.

That is what Thomas actually left behind, and it is the thing worth imitating: not the conclusions, but the machine for arriving at them in public.
