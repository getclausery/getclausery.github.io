---
title: The Lexicon
lede: Inculturation is won or lost at the level of vocabulary. Thirty words carry the manifest space — fifteen at length, fifteen in brief — and most of them are equivocal, and the equivocation is where the metaphysics hides.
---

There is a reason the Church's missionary history is full of fights about words. *Filioque.* *Theotokos.* *Substance.* *Person.* The fights were not about pedantry; they were about whether a word could carry the faith without smuggling in a rival metaphysics. A term borrowed from the surrounding culture is a vessel, and vessels have shapes, and a shape that is wrong will deform whatever is poured into it.

The manifest space has a vocabulary of its own, and it is a good vocabulary — vivid, concrete, emotionally intelligent, and almost entirely equivocal. Each of its key words carries two meanings at once: a Catholic meaning, which is usually true, and a manifest-space meaning, which usually is not. The person using the word is usually holding both, and does not know it.

This chapter takes the most important of those words and does four things with each: gives the manifest-space meaning, names the equivocation, gives the Catholic transposition, and issues a verdict.

::scene

**Manifest.** *Manifest-space meaning:* to cause a desired thing to appear in the external world by interior work. *Equivocation:* the word in Christian usage is passive and divine — Christ "was manifested in the flesh" (1 Tim 3:16); the Spirit is "manifested" for the common good (1 Cor 12:7); the children of God will be "manifested" (Rom 8:19); the believer's life is "hidden with Christ" until it is manifested in glory (Col 3:1–4). In every case the manifesting is done by God, or done to the believer, and never by the believer to the world. *Transposition:* keep the word and reverse the direction. *Verdict:* retain, re-oriented.

**Abundance.** *Manifest-space meaning:* material and experiential plenty, measured in goods. *Equivocation:* the Gospel's abundance is the superabundance of grace — "I came that they may have life, and have it abundantly" (John 10:10) — which is compatible with poverty, and which the saints have usually experienced in poverty. *Transposition:* abundance is a property of the gift, not of the inventory. *Verdict:* correct.

**Alignment.** *Manifest-space meaning:* the matching of one's inner state to a desired outcome. *Equivocation:* the tradition's word is conformity — the conforming of the will to God's — and its model is Gethsemane, where the alignment was perfect and the outcome was the Cross. *Transposition:* alignment is with a person, not with an outcome. *Verdict:* correct.

**Block.** *Manifest-space meaning:* an interior obstruction preventing the result. *Equivocation:* the tradition has three quite different words for what stops a person: sin (which is chosen), disordered attachment (which is habitual), and illness (which is suffered). Collapsing all three into "block" makes the first two invisible and the third a moral failing. *Transposition:* name which of the three you are dealing with. *Verdict:* refuse, replace with the three.

**Energy.** *Manifest-space meaning:* a quasi-physical force transmitted by thought and feeling. *Equivocation:* *energeia* in the tradition means act, operation, activity — the operation of a nature — and in the Greek Fathers the uncreated energies of God are God himself, not a force in the world. *Transposition:* there are real energies: grace, charism, virtue, the *virtus* of a sacrament. They are personal and gratuitous, and none of them is measured in units. *Verdict:* refuse as substance, retain as act.

**Frequency, vibration.** *Manifest-space meaning:* the medium by which like attracts like. *Equivocation:* metaphor borrowed from acoustics, used as though it were physics. *Transposition:* none — there is nothing to transpose, because there is no referent. The underlying experience (that a person's habitual state is perceptible and contagious) is real and has better names: temperament, habit, atmosphere, the *sensus fidei* of a community. *Verdict:* refuse.

**The universe.** *Manifest-space meaning:* an impersonal-responsive agency that hears, answers, and arranges. *Equivocation:* "the universe" in Catholic speech means creation, which is not an agent and cannot hear. The functions assigned to it belong to providence, which is personal, free, and cruciform. *Transposition:* creation is real, providence is real, and neither is a mechanism with a mood. *Verdict:* correct, sharply.

**Higher self.** *Manifest-space meaning:* the divine core of the person, the true identity beneath the ego. *Equivocation:* the tradition has *imago Dei* (a real dignity, wounded by sin), conscience (a real voice, which can be mistaken), and the indwelling Spirit (a real presence, which is not a part of the self). None of them is a "higher self," and all of them are better news, because all of them involve someone who is not me. *Transposition:* the self is not deep enough to save itself. *Verdict:* refuse.

**Shadow.** *Manifest-space meaning:* the disowned material of the psyche, which must be integrated. *Equivocation:* there is a real phenomenon here — the tradition calls it the passions, the wounds, the *fomes*, the tinder of sin — and a real practice, which is the examination of conscience and the confession of what one would rather not see. But the Jungian frame makes the shadow a *project* of integration, whereas the Christian frame makes it a *mercy* to be confessed. *Transposition:* you do not integrate the shadow; you bring it into the light and it is judged and healed. *Verdict:* correct.

**Limiting belief.** *Manifest-space meaning:* the sole obstacle to the desired outcome. *Equivocation:* some beliefs are indeed false and limiting, and the tradition cares about them (it calls the cure *faith*, and the disease *despair*). But the frame makes every limit a belief, and therefore makes creaturely limits — finitude, illness, death, other people's freedom — into errors of the mind. *Transposition:* there are real limits and there are false beliefs, and the discernment between them is most of the spiritual life. *Verdict:* correct.

**Affirmation.** *Manifest-space meaning:* a formula repeated to impress the subconscious. *Equivocation:* the tradition's parallel is the aspiration — a short prayer, often scriptural, repeated through the day ("Jesus, have mercy on me"). The difference is the addressee: an affirmation speaks into a mechanism; an aspiration speaks to a person. *Transposition:* keep the repetition, change the object. *Verdict:* correct.

**Scripting.** *Manifest-space meaning:* writing the desired future in the present tense as though it had happened. *Equivocation:* the tradition's parallel is the written petition and the spiritual journal — both real practices with a long history, and both addressed to God rather than to the subconscious. *Transposition:* write it, date it, and offer it. Then, when it is answered or refused, write that down too; the record of God's dealings with you is worth more than the record of your wishes. *Verdict:* correct.

**Detachment.** *Manifest-space meaning:* not needing the outcome, so that the outcome can arrive. *Equivocation:* the tradition's word is *indiferencia* — Ignatius's "indifference," which is not apathy but a freedom from inordinate attachment such that one is ready to receive either health or sickness, riches or poverty, provided it serves God's glory (*SpEx* 23). The manifest version is a technique for getting the thing; the Ignatian version is a freedom from needing it. *Transposition:* detachment is not a strategy. *Verdict:* correct, and the correction is the whole difference.

**Signs.** *Manifest-space meaning:* confirmations from the universe. *Equivocation:* the tradition reads signs too — but by discernment, with criteria, under obedience, and with a strong bias against novelty. *Transposition:* the rules for the discernment of spirits (*SpEx* 313–336) are a tested method for exactly this, and they are better than 11:11. *Verdict:* correct.

**Gratitude.** *Manifest-space meaning:* a technique that raises vibration and attracts more. *Equivocation:* gratitude in the tradition is a virtue — a habit of the will by which one acknowledges a debt to a giver — and its highest form is the Eucharist. *Transposition:* gratitude requires a someone. *Verdict:* integrate, with the object restored.

::scene

A second tier of words can be handled more briefly, but each has the same structure — a true experience, a false frame, a transposable core.

| Word | Manifest-space sense | Catholic disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| intention | a directed force that shapes events | a real act of the will, ordered to a real good — keep, disenchant |
| focus | concentration that magnetizes | attention as a moral act — keep |
| beliefs | the operating system of reality | faith as assent, opinion, and trust — distinguish three things |
| self-concept | the identity that determines outcomes | the self as gift received, not project undertaken |
| resistance | interior opposition to the good | sin, fear, or illness — name which |
| release | letting go of blocks | surrender to providence — keep, re-target |
| surrender | giving up control so results arrive | *fiat*, the consent of Mary — keep, deepen |
| worthiness | the vibration of deserving | grace, which is not deserved and is therefore better |
| journey | the arc of self-development | *viator*, the pilgrim in via — keep |
| awakening | the realization of one's divinity | conversion, which is a turning, not an unfolding |
| abundance mindset | the belief in unlimited supply | trust in providence and detachment from goods |
| protection | energetic shielding | the sacramentals, the sign of the cross, the name of Jesus |
| the field | an invisible medium that carries intention and answers it | there is no such medium: creatures act through their natures, and God acts through providence — the word names the hole where a doctrine of creation should be |
| divine timing | the delay before the universe delivers | providence and patience: the delay is not the technique refining itself but the place where hope is formed (2 Pet 3:8) |
| visualization | the core technique: see it as done, and it becomes so | the Ignatian composition of place (*SpEx* 47): imagination as the way into a mystery that is given, not a way of manufacturing an outcome |

::scene

One observation about this chapter, and it is the reason it exists.

If you remove the manifest space's vocabulary from a person and give her nothing in its place, you have not converted her; you have silenced her. She will still have the experiences — the sense that her interior life is connected to the world, the sense that the world is addressed to her, the sense that words matter, the sense that gratitude changes her — and she will have no words for them, and the experiences will either fade or return under a different brand.

The Church's vocabulary is better. It is older, more precise, more beautiful, and it has been tested in every century by people who were about to die. But it is not available to a person who has never heard it, and it is not attractive when it is delivered as a correction.

So the lexicon is not a weapon. It is a translation table, and the work of translation is the work of a culture — slow, patient, and done one conversation at a time.
