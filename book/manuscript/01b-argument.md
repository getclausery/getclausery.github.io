title: The Argument on One Page
---

If you read only one page of this book, read this one. Everything that follows is the working-out.

<div class="thesis">
<p>The manifest space is not primarily a set of false beliefs. It is a set of true observations attached to a false engine.</p>
</div>

That sentence is the whole book. The observations are real: attention shapes perception, expectation shapes action, habit hardens into character, words do things, the interior life is not private. The engine is not: a universe that answers to the intensity of your feeling, that has no one at the centre of it, and that bills you for your own suffering.

::scene

**The argument in eight steps.**

**1. This is not a fringe.** It is a grammar that a generation learned before it learned the Creed — a vocabulary, a set of procedures, and a mood, distributed by feed and group chat. It is the first spiritual system most of its adherents ever met that told them their inner life mattered (chapters 1–2).

**2. It works, partly, because it is partly true.** Four of its observations are secure, and the Church has taught all four for longer than the movement has existed. What fails is the causal architecture — and the failure is not cosmetic, because it makes the creature into the creator and the sufferer into the author of her own suffering (chapters 3–4).

**3. The Church has done this before.** In the thirteenth century, Thomas Aquinas took a complete rival account of reality apart and rebuilt it inside the faith. He did it in six moves, he rejected about a third of what he received by name, it got him condemned, and the comparison has four limits that the book states rather than hides (chapters 5–7).

**4. Four tests sort any spiritual claim:** who is creator and who is creature; what is nature and what is grace; what is the cross and what is glory; who is this for — the self, or the Church (chapters 8–12).

**5. Fourteen central claims, fourteen verdicts:** four to integrate, three to correct, seven to reject. The verdicts are stated one by one, with reasons, so that a reader who disagrees knows exactly where to disagree (chapters 13–15).

**6. The word is already ours.** *Manifestation* is a divine act before it is a human technique. What the manifest space offers is a parody of a structure the Church owns — glory, provision, desire, hiddenness, sacrament, communion (chapters 16–24).

**7. So keep the technique and change the addressee.** Six marks distinguish a Catholic practice from a Christian-looking technique; four sets of transpositions convert the actual methods; one worked rite, the Novena of Desire, can be prayed as written; and two questions do most of the work at the bedside (chapters 25–32).

**8. Then build, measure, and wait.** Listen before you refute, publish the disputed questions, build the institutions, judge by fruit rather than testimonial, and give it fifty years. Answer the objections from both sides honestly — and where an objection cannot be answered, say so (chapters 33–40).

::scene

**The toolkit.** Eight instruments, each stated in one line, each portable enough to be used by someone who has not read the book.

| Instrument | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **The Seven Rules** | The manifest space's grammar, stated in its own terms — refute one rule and six remain | ch. 2 |
| **The Six Moves** | Aquinas's method of reconstruction, translated into the work this book attempts | ch. 6 |
| **The Four Tests** | Creator, grace, cross, Church — four questions that sort any claim | chs. 8–12 |
| **The Fourteen Verdicts** | Integrate, correct, reject — applied claim by claim, with reasons | chs. 13–14 |
| **The Six Marks** | What makes a practice Catholic rather than a technique with a veneer | ch. 26 |
| **Keep the Technique, Change the Addressee** | The pastoral rule: take up the method, redirect the one it is addressed to | chs. 27–30 |
| **The Two Questions** | Ninety seconds of triage, three colours, one rule about the bedside | ch. 32 |
| **Fruit, Not Testimonials** | How to judge a practice without lying to yourself about the evidence | ch. 36 |

<div class="thesis">
<p>If the argument is right, then the question is not whether the Church can survive the manifest space. It is whether the Church will do, with a generation formed by it, what she once did with Aristotle: describe it honestly, refuse what is false, keep what is true, and build something better that outlasts the argument.</p>
</div>
