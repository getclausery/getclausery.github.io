---
title: Conclusion: The Manifestation of the Children of God
lede: Return to the girl at eleven-eleven. What to say to her, in four sentences — and why the whole world is waiting for the thing she is actually looking for.
---

We began in a bedroom at 11:11 with a notebook and a girl who had been told that her thoughts could move the world, and who had begun, somewhere in the year her mother was ill, to be afraid of her own mind.

Everything in this book has been an attempt to answer her.

Not to refute her — she has not made an argument. Not to correct her — she has not made a mistake, exactly, or rather she has made the mistake that her whole culture made and handed to her in a beautiful package. But to answer the question she was actually asking when she opened the notebook, which was not *how do I get what I want* but **is anything listening, and am I to blame**.

::scene

Here is what I would say to her, if I had an hour and she would let me. Four sentences, and then a practice.

**First: you are not creating reality, and that is good news.** You are cooperating in a reality you did not make, which means that the disaster is not your fault and the good thing is not your achievement and the world is not a mirror of your worst thought. The weight you have been carrying is not yours, and you can put it down.

**Second: your desire is real, and it is not an instruction from the universe.** It is a signpost, and signposts can be read wrongly, and the reading is what a life of prayer is for. What you want matters. It matters enough that it needs to be purified, which is a different thing from being suppressed, and much better.

**Third: there is someone listening, and he can say no.** That sounds like bad news and it is the best news in the world, because a someone who can say no is a someone who can love you, and a law that always says yes is a machine, and a machine cannot love you and cannot be loved and cannot forgive you, and you need to be forgiven much more than you need to be obeyed.

**Fourth: what you are actually waiting for is not the thing in the notebook.** It is the manifestation of the children of God — the revealing of what you are, in glory, with the wounds still in you and the body restored. The whole creation is on tiptoe for it. It is not available by technique. It has been promised by someone who keeps his promises.

And then a practice: five minutes, tonight, before you sleep. Say the name of Jesus, slowly, and then say the thing you actually want, and then say *not my will but yours*, and then go to sleep. Do it tomorrow. Do it badly. Do it for a month, and then come and tell someone what happened.

::scene

Now, having said that to her, it is worth saying what the book has been saying to us.

The manifest space is not a heresy in the ordinary sense. It is a *parody*, and a parody is the most instructive thing there is, because it shows you the shape of the original by distorting it. Look at what it has reproduced, in a distorted form:

- It has a doctrine of **providence** — the universe responds — with the person removed.
- It has a doctrine of **creation** — you make your world — with the givenness removed.
- It has a doctrine of **grace** — alignment, abundance, flow — with the giver removed.
- It has a doctrine of **revelation** — the universe is always speaking — with the word removed.
- It has a doctrine of **sacrament** — the method, the ritual, the object — with Christ removed.
- It has a doctrine of **communion** — energy, connection, oneness — with the other removed.
- It has a doctrine of **glory** — manifestation, abundance, the wish fulfilled — with the Cross removed.

Every one of those is a Christian doctrine with its centre cut out. That is why the system feels, to the people inside it, so familiar and so right, and why it is so hard to argue with: it is not a foreign religion. It is *our* religion, in a version that has had the person of God removed from it.

Which means the answer is not to build a new thing. It is to restore the centre — the person — to a structure that is already there, and to trust that when the centre is restored, the whole thing will come right, because a parody is healed by the original and not by a refutation.

::scene

There is a temptation at the end of a book like this to make a large claim for it. I will make a small one.

If a reader puts this book down and does three things, it will have done its work. If she learns one short prayer and says it at a fixed time every day. If she finds one person to whom she can manifest her conscience — a confessor, a director, a friend who is wise. If she keeps one honest record of her petitions and God's answers, including the refusals.

Three things: a practice, a witness, a record. They are not impressive. They are what the Church has always given to a person who asks how to begin, and they are enough, over a lifetime, to produce the only result that matters.

::scene

The book ends where the faith ends, which is not at a conclusion but at a promise.

"For the creation waits with eager longing for the revealing of the children of God" (Rom 8:19). The word is *apokalypsis*: an unveiling, a manifestation. It is the thing the manifest space has been reaching for with the wrong tools, and the thing the Church has been carrying in a tabernacle, and the thing that the world is actually groaning for, whether it knows it or not.

It has not happened yet. Your life, if you are a Christian, is hidden with Christ in God, and it will be manifested, and the manifestation will not be a technique but a resurrection, and it will not be your achievement but your adoption, and it will be given to you in a body, in a communion, in a world made new, with the wounds still in it and the glory coming through them.

Until then: pray. Ask for what you want. Say *not my will*. Go to confession. Keep the record. Sit with the sick and do not explain their suffering to them. Refuse the systems that tell them it is their fault. Give away whatever you learn.

And when you are tempted to think that nothing is happening, remember the girl at 11:11, and the woman who wrote nine times a day for a year and did not get what she asked for, and the fact that she is still there, in a bedroom, hoping — because hope is the one thing the manifest space could never manufacture and could never destroy, and it is the one thing that the Church has always known how to keep.
