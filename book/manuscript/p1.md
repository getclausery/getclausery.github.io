Before anything can be harmonized, two things must be understood: the thing that is to be harmonized, and the operation by which the Church has harmonized anything like it before. This part supplies both. It describes the manifest space from the inside — its grammar, its implicit theology, and the real human needs it serves — and then returns to Paris in the thirteenth century to recover, in some detail, the method by which Thomas Aquinas took a rival account of reality apart and rebuilt it inside the faith. It ends with the cost of that method, which was considerable, and which anyone attempting the same thing should expect to pay.
