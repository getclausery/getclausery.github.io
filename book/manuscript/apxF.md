kind: appendix
title: Note on the Text
lede: How this book was made, how to correct it, and what it is not.
---

**Status.** This is a first edition, not an approved publication, and it is a theological research project rather than a settled statement of doctrine. It has no *imprimatur*, no *nihil obstat*, and no ecclesiastical mandate of any kind: it is not authoritative, and it does not ask to be treated as though it were. It was written with the help of the Holy Spirit — which is a claim about help received and not about authority conferred, and the two are worth keeping apart, because the second is a bishop's to make and the first is not. It is submitted to the judgment of the Church, and any reader who finds an error should assume that the error is the author's and not the faith's.

**Provenance.** The manuscript is written in plain text, one file per chapter, and built by a small script into four editions: a single-file reading edition; a print edition set for 5.5 × 8.5 inch pages; an EPUB 3 file with a navigation document and cover; and a typeset PDF with running heads, page numbers, and a contents page whose numbers are generated from the finished book. The word count and the page estimate printed at the end of the reading edition are generated by the build, not asserted by the author, and they change every time a chapter is added. The PDF is the only edition in which the pages are fixed, and therefore the only one in which "one page" means what it says.

**Citations.** References are given in the text rather than in footnotes, in a short form: Aquinas by part, question, and article (*ST* I-II, q. 49, a. 1); magisterial documents by paragraph; the *Exercises* by paragraph number (*SpEx* 23); the *Catechism* by paragraph (CCC 2111). Appendix D gives the editions relied on.

**Composite portraits.** The named persons in the early chapters are composites; their details have been combined and altered, and no composite corresponds to a single identifiable individual. Direct quotations from published authors are real and are cited. Where a number appears, it is drawn from a published source; where the author is reasoning from impressions, the text says so.

**What this book is not.** It is not a diagnosis of any individual. It is not a substitute for spiritual direction, confession, or clinical care. It is not an argument that a Christian may practise the law of attraction — the verdicts in chapter 13 are mostly *reject*. And it is not the last word: it is a first attempt at a method, offered in the hope that others will do it better.

**How to correct it.** The most useful corrections are of four kinds: a factual error about a source; a misdescription of a practice by someone who does it; a doctrinal error identified with a citation; and a report of a pilot — including a failed one. A book of this kind is worth exactly as much as it is corrigible, and the corrigibility has to be real, which means that the corrections have to be able to change the text.

**Acknowledgements.** To the priests and catechists who answered questions they were not trained for; to the people who described their practices without requiring that they be admired; to the tradition that has been doing this work for two thousand years, and to the saints who did it without writing anything down.
