Free event planning contract template (Word)

A contract between an event planner and a client defining the planning services, scope, fees, vendor coordination, payment schedule and cancellation terms for an event.

An event planning contract covers the services an event planner will provide for a wedding, corporate event, party or conference — venue scouting, vendor management, timeline, day-of coordination, budget management and communication.

Be precise about scope: 'full planning' vs 'day-of coordination' are very different engagements. Set limits on hours, revisions and meetings to avoid scope creep.

What to include#

  • Client and planner. Event planner name/business; client name(s); event date(s) and venue (if known); date of agreement.
  • Scope of services. Specific services included (venue search, vendor coordination, budget management, design, day-of coordination, RSVP tracking) and what is excluded (catering, venue fees, decor costs).
  • Fees and payment. Flat fee, percentage of budget, or hourly rate; deposit/retainer amount; payment milestones; reimbursement policy for expenses; any referral commissions disclosed.
  • Client responsibilities. Timely decisions, budget approvals, vendor payments outside planner scope, guest list management, and accessibility requirements.
  • Cancellation, liability and IP. Cancellation/postponement terms and refund schedule; force majeure; liability limits; insurance; photo rights for portfolio; and governing law.

How to fill it in#

  1. Step 1: Define the package clearly

    List exactly what is included — full planning, partial, month-of, or day-of coordination.

  2. Step 2: Disclose fees and commissions

    State how you are paid (flat fee, percentage, referral commissions); transparency builds trust and complies with industry standards.

  3. Step 3: Set a revision/meeting limit

    Include a number of included meetings/revisions to avoid endless scope.

  4. Step 4: Specify payment milestones

    Deposit on signing, progress payments leading up to the event, final balance due before the event day.

  5. Step 5: Include force majeure

    Pandemics, weather, venue closures — state what happens to deposits/fees if the event is postponed or canceled by forces outside either party control.",

Questions

What percentage do planners typically charge?

10-20% of total event budget for full planning; flat fees or day-of rates are also common.

Do I need a separate contract for day-of coordination?

Yes — day-of coordination is a different scope; use a shorter agreement listing the specific coordination duties.

What about vendor deposits?

Clarify whether clients pay vendors directly or through the planner; you are not a bank unless explicitly stated.

Who is liable for vendor no-shows?

Planner should act in good faith but not guarantee third parties; liability limits and force majeure help.

What if the client cancels?

Non-refundable deposit; sliding-scale refund based on how far out cancellation happens.
