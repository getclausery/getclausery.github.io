What is in the rent increase letter

  • Date, tenant names and property address
  • Current rent, new rent and start date
  • Month-to-month notice, or the end of the lease term
  • Optional renewal offer on the same terms
  • Optional reason for the increase
  • Everything else stays the same
  • Optional date to reply by
  • Contact details and signature

[Tenant names]

[Property address]

Re: Rent increase for [Property address]

Dear [Tenant names],

This letter is formal notice that the rent for [Property address] will increase from [Current rent] to [New rent] a month, starting on [Effective date].

Your tenancy is month to month, and this letter gives you [Notice days] days' notice before the new rent applies.Your current lease term ends on [Lease end date], and the new rent applies after that, under the renewal we are offering you on otherwise the same terms. This letter gives you [Notice days] days' notice.

The increase reflects [Increase reason].

Everything else about your tenancy stays the same, including how and when rent is paid. Please let us know by [Reply by date] whether you plan to stay.

If you have any questions, please contact [Landlord contact name] at [Landlord phone] or [Landlord email]. Thank you for being our tenant.

Sincerely,

____________________________

[Landlord name]

Questions

How much notice is needed for a rent increase?

It depends on where the property is and the type of tenancy. During a fixed-term lease the rent usually cannot rise until the lease ends, unless the lease allows it. For month-to-month tenancies many states require 30 days, and some require more for larger increases: in California, for example, an increase of more than 10% needs 90 days' notice.

Are there limits on how much I can raise the rent?

In some places. Some cities have rent control or rent stabilization, and a few states, including California and Oregon, cap yearly increases for many rental homes. Check the rules for your property before you send the letter.

Do I have to give a reason?

Usually not, but a short reason, such as higher property taxes or insurance, helps keep a good tenant. The reason paragraph is optional.
