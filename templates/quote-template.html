Free price quote template (Word)

A formal document from a seller to a prospective customer stating a fixed price for specified goods or services, with breakdown, terms, and an expiration date (validity period) by which the customer must accept.

A price quote (or quotation) tells a customer how much you will charge for specific goods or services. It is fixed for a stated period (commonly 30 days) so the customer can accept, after which you provide the goods/service and invoice.

Quotes differ from estimates (which are approximate). Once accepted a quote usually forms the basis of the contract. Itemise clearly so there is no ambiguity about scope.

What to include#

  • Business and customer. Your business name, logo, address and contact; customer name, address and contact; quote number and date.
  • Scope / items. Description of each line item (goods or services), quantity, unit price, line totals, and any assumptions or inclusions/exclusions.
  • Pricing totals. Subtotal, discounts, taxes (VAT/GST/sales tax), shipping/expenses if known, and total price.
  • Terms and validity. How long the quote is valid (e.g. 30 days), payment terms (deposit required, milestones, net terms), delivery timeline, and any exclusions.
  • Acceptance. Statement that the customer can accept by signing and returning or paying the deposit; space for customer signature/date.

How to fill it in#

  1. Step 1: Understand the scope

    Clarify requirements before quoting — misquoted scope kills profit.

  2. Step 2: Itemise, do not lump

    Break out labour, materials, expenses and taxes so the customer sees what they are paying for.

  3. Step 3: State assumptions

    List anything assumed (access, client-provided materials, working hours) so they can be challenged before acceptance.

  4. Step 4: Set a validity period

    Quotes typically expire in 14-30 days to protect you from material-price changes and schedule drift.

  5. Step 5: Get acceptance in writing

    Have the client sign and return (or pay deposit) before starting work; issue an invoice against the accepted quote.

Questions

Quote vs estimate?

A quote is a fixed price the customer can accept; an estimate is an educated guess that may change. Use quotes for well-defined work.

How long should it be valid?

14-30 days is standard; shorter for volatile material prices or time-sensitive offers.

Can I change the price after acceptance?

Only via a change order agreed in writing; otherwise the accepted price is the contract price.

Should I include tax?

Yes — show net, tax and gross so the customer knows the total; customers hate surprise tax.

Is a verbal quote binding?

Harder to prove — always put quotes in writing to avoid disputes.
