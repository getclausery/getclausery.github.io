Free expense reimbursement form template (Word)

A standard form employees use to request reimbursement for work-related expenses they paid out of pocket — travel, meals, supplies, mileage — with receipts attached and coding for accounting.

An expense reimbursement form is how employees submit out-of-pocket work expenses for repayment: travel, client meals, mileage, supplies, conference fees, or other approved business costs. It itemises each expense, attaches receipts, codes to the right budget/category and gets manager approval before payment.

Many US states require employers to reimburse necessary business expenses (e.g. California Labor Code 2802). A standard form creates the paper trail required for tax compliance and reimbursement.

What to include#

  • Employee info. Employee name, department, manager, employee ID, pay period/date submitted.
  • Expense details per line. Date of expense, description/purpose, category (travel, meals, mileage, supplies, lodging, other), amount, and whether it is billable to a client/project.
  • Mileage (if applicable). From/to locations, miles driven, purpose; multiply by the standard mileage rate (IRS HMRC per-km).
  • Receipts and coding. Receipts attached (original or scanned), accounting/GL code, project/client if billable; totals and any cash advance to offset.
  • Approvals. Employee signature, manager approval signature (and finance approval if over threshold), approval date, and date paid.

How to fill it in#

  1. Step 1: Submit promptly

    Submit expenses within the company deadline (often monthly or within 30 days).

  2. Step 2: Itemise each expense

    One line per expense; do not lump together; label personal portions.

  3. Step 3: Attach receipts

    Attach original or scanned receipts — credit card statements alone are not always sufficient; keep copies.

  4. Step 4: Code correctly

    Apply the right GL/cost centre; client-billable expenses get a client code.

  5. Step 5: Manager approval

    Manager reviews for business purpose and policy compliance, signs and submits to payroll/AP for reimbursement.

Questions

Are receipts required?

Company policy determines the threshold; IRS requires receipts for expenditures over $75. Always require itemised receipts for meals/hotel.

Is reimbursement taxable?

If made under an accountable plan (business connection, substantiated, excess returned), reimbursements are not wages and not taxed.

Do I have to reimburse employee expenses?

Some states (e.g. California) require reimbursement of all necessary business expenses; best practice everywhere.

What about mileage?

Reimburse at or below the IRS standard mileage rate for business miles; require a log showing business purpose, date and miles.

Can I refuse non-policy expenses?

Yes — if the expense violates policy (alcohol without client, unapproved travel) it can be declined.
