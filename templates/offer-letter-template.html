Job offer letter

Reviewed by Clausery editors · 31 October 2026 · 2 min read

A formal written offer of employment: role title, start date, salary, pay schedule, benefits, at-will status (US), contingencies and a deadline to accept.

A job offer letter confirms in writing what was discussed verbally and is signed by the candidate to accept. Detailed terms belong in an employee handbook or employment agreement.

Keep it short: state the role, compensation, start date, contingencies and deadline to sign.

What to include#

  • Role and start date. Job title, department, reporting manager, start date, status and work location.
  • Compensation. Salary/hourly rate, pay frequency, bonus/commission and equity if offered.
  • Benefits. High-level health, retirement, PTO, parental leave summary — reference plan documents for detail.
  • Contingencies. Background check, drug test, references, I-9/right-to-work, signed employment agreement.
  • At-will and deadline. At-will language for US roles; 3–7 day deadline to sign.

How to fill it in#

  1. Step 1: Confirm verbally first

    Extend a verbal offer before sending the letter.

  2. Step 2: State compensation clearly

    Salary/rate, pay schedule, signing/relocation bonus and first pay date.

  3. Step 3: Name contingencies

    Background check, references, right-to-work and drug tests.

  4. Step 4: Add at-will and deadline

    At-will language (US); 3–5 days to sign.

  5. Step 5: Send and track

    Email as PDF with Word attached; keep the signed copy in the employee file.

Questions

Binding?

Signed offer is a contract; US roles typically at-will. Contingencies must clear.

Need separate contract?

For most employees offer letter + handbook is enough.

Rescind signed offer?

Generally yes in at-will states; reliance claims possible.

Time to accept?

3–7 days standard.

Detail benefits?

Summary is enough; reference full plan documents.
