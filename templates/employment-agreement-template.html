A contract between an employer and employee setting out the terms of employment — role, compensation, benefits, work hours, intellectual property, confidentiality and how employment can end.

An employment agreement (or employment contract) is signed when a new hire starts work. It sets out the key terms of the job — title, pay, hours, benefits, leave, confidentiality, IP assignment, non-compete/non-solicit and termination — so both sides know where they stand.

At-will employment is the default in most US states, but a written agreement is still useful for IP assignment, confidentiality, bonus/commission terms, and any restrictive covenants. Have an attorney review restrictive covenants as enforceability varies widely by state.

What to include#

  • Parties and role. Employer name and address; employee name and address; job title; department; start date; reports-to; work location (and remote-work terms).
  • Compensation. Base salary or hourly rate, pay frequency, overtime eligibility, bonus/commission structure, equity if any, paydays.
  • Benefits and leave. Health insurance, retirement plan, PTO/sick leave, holidays, parental leave, and other benefits (and when eligibility starts).
  • Duties and expectations. Core responsibilities, work hours, at-will or fixed-term status, performance review schedule, and any probation period.
  • Legal clauses. Intellectual property assignment, confidentiality, non-compete/non-solicit (as enforceable), return of property, dispute resolution (arbitration/mediation), and termination terms.

How to fill it in#

  1. Step 1: Finalise the offer first

    Agree on salary, title, start date and key terms verbally or in an offer letter before drafting the agreement.

  2. Step 2: Use compliant language

    Make sure restrictive covenants meet your state rules (non-competes are banned or unenforceable for many workers post-2024).

  3. Step 3: Spell out compensation clearly

    State base pay, bonus formula (discretionary vs formulaic), commission triggers and paydays explicitly.

  4. Step 4: Include IP and confidentiality

    Assign work product to the company and require confidentiality of company information; include a return-of-property clause.

  5. Step 5: Sign before day one

    Both parties sign before or on the start date; employee gets a fully signed copy. File with HR records.

Questions

Is an employment agreement required?

No in the US (at-will is the default) but strongly recommended for IP assignment, confidentiality and clear terms.

Can I change it after signing?

Only with the employee written consent; material changes without consent can be unenforceable.

Are non-competes enforceable?

They are banned for most workers under the 2024 FTC rule (currently under litigation) and heavily restricted in many states; consult counsel.

Do I need one for every employee?

It is best practice for exempt/professional roles and any role with IP access; offer letters can suffice for hourly staff if you cover IP/confidentiality.

At-will vs fixed-term?

At-will means either side can end it any time for a lawful reason; fixed-term sets an end date and usually requires cause or notice to terminate early.
