What is in the photo and model release

  • Model, photographer and optional client
  • Shoot date, location and optional project
  • Commercial use in any media, or only listed uses
  • Optional time limit on use
  • Use of the model's name, or no identification
  • Editing and ownership, with optional restrictions
  • Paid release, or release for other value such as images
  • Release of privacy and publicity claims
  • Parent or guardian consent for a model under 18
  • Signatures and optional witness

Questions

When do I need a model release?

Generally when a recognisable person appears in a photo or video used commercially, such as advertising, marketing or stock. Editorial uses such as news often do not need one, but privacy and publicity rules vary by country and state, and stock agencies usually require a signed release anyway.

Can a minor sign a model release?

A minor usually cannot give a binding release alone, so a parent or legal guardian should sign too. Tick the under-18 box and the template adds a guardian consent paragraph and signature line.

Is this the same as a photography contract?

No. A photography contract is between the photographer and the client who books the shoot. A model release is signed by the person photographed, giving permission to use their image. Many shoots need both.
