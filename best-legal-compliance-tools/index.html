Best legal and compliance tools for startups and small businesses

The tools founders actually use#

Each entry links to the provider’s own site so you can confirm current features, plans and prices. No competitor price or rating is quoted here because none of it can be verified from this repository.

Legal document templates (Word)

Clausery#

Contracts, NDAs, offer letters, invoices, quotes and receipts as editable Word files.

Why people use it: The whole library is free to fill in online and download, with no account and no card. Documents are assembled in your browser, so nothing is uploaded. You can also add your own Word templates and automate up to three of them free.

Who it is for: Startup founders, freelancers and small businesses that need clean, editable agreements and billing documents without a drafting subscription.

The honest catch: It prepares documents; it does not collect signatures or payments, host a client portal, or give legal advice. Templates are general samples you review and adapt.

Official site • Try it

\n

Legal document library

LawDepot#

Jurisdiction-specific legal forms and documents (leases, wills, bills of sale, contracts).

Why people use it: A large library of prescribed forms with state/country-specific wording, so you start from a document built for your location rather than a blank page.

Who it is for: Buyers who need a form tailored to a particular jurisdiction and are comfortable with an account-based download flow.

The honest catch: Download terms, purchase options and renewal conditions vary by document; confirm the exact form and any charges before relying on it.

Official site • Try it

\n

Documents plus attorney access

Rocket Lawyer#

Legal documents plus document-review questions and attorney consultations as part of a membership.

Why people use it: Combines document generation with access to legal-help services, so a buyer can get both a file and a professional opinion in one subscription.

Who it is for: Buyers who want document drafting together with the option of attorney review, not just the template itself.

The honest catch: The legal-help element is a membership service with its own terms, cancellation rules and eligibility; separate it from the document cost when comparing.

Official site • Try it

\n

Startup entity formation

Clerky#

Delaware C-Corp incorporation, stock issuance, 83(b) elections and other startup-formation paperwork.

Why people use it: Built specifically for early-stage startups incorporating and issuing equity, with the standard formation documents founders actually need.

Who it is for: Startup founders setting up a US entity and equity paperwork for the first time.

The honest catch: Focused on US formation and corporate paperwork, not day-to-day contracts, NDAs or invoices; you still need a separate tool for those documents.

Official site • Try it

\n

Startup entity formation

Stripe Atlas#

US company formation (Delaware or other), plus banking, tax ID and payment-processing setup for internet businesses.

Why people use it: Packages incorporation with a bank account, tax ID and Stripe integration, aimed at founders launching an online business quickly.

Who it is for: Founders launching an internet or SaaS startup who want formation and payments set up together.

The honest catch: A formation and banking package, not an ongoing contract or document-drafting tool; separate from day-to-day legal paperwork.

Official site • Try it

\n

E-signature and document workflow

PandaDoc#

Proposals, quotes and contracts with electronic signatures, approvals and CRM connections.

Why people use it: A connected workflow around a document: tracking when a customer views or signs, approval steps and sales-tool integrations.

Who it is for: Sales teams and businesses whose main need is getting a document signed and tracked, not just producing an editable file.

The honest catch: Signature, tracking and approval features are plan-dependent; the core value is the workflow around the document rather than the Word file itself.

Official site • Try it

\n

Electronic signature

HelloSign (Dropbox Sign)#

Electronic signatures and signature-request tracking for contracts and agreements.

Why people use it: A focused e-signature tool: upload a document, send it for signature, and keep an audit trail of who signed and when.

Who it is for: Teams that already have documents ready and just need a reliable way to get them signed electronically.

The honest catch: It signs documents; it does not draft them, so you still need a separate source for the contract wording.

Official site • Try it

\n

Electronic signature

DocuSign#

Electronic signatures and agreement management at enterprise scale.

Why people use it: The most widely adopted e-signature platform, with extensive integrations, authentication options and agreement lifecycle features.

Who it is for: Established businesses and enterprises that need broad integrations and advanced agreement-management controls.

The honest catch: Enterprise-oriented pricing and complexity; often more than a small business or founder needs for occasional signing.

Official site • Try it

\n

Business formation and legal documents

LegalZoom#

Business formation (LLC, corporation), registered agent, and a library of legal documents and contracts.

Why people use it: One of the best-known formation services: sets up an LLC or corporation, provides a registered agent, and offers document templates plus attorney-access add-ons.

Who it is for: Founders who want a well-known brand to handle incorporation and basic legal documents in one place.

The honest catch: Pricier than doing formation yourself; document templates are general and sold à la carte or via subscription. Confirm what each plan actually includes before paying.

Official site • Try it

\n

Startup entity formation

ZenBusiness#

LLC and corporation formation, registered agent, annual report and compliance reminders for small businesses.

Why people use it: Formation-focused service with registered agent and compliance calendar, aimed at small businesses staying on top of filing deadlines.

Who it is for: Founders who want formation plus ongoing compliance reminders in one subscription.

The honest catch: Compliance help is reminders and filings, not legal advice; you still need a separate tool for contracts, NDAs and invoices.

Official site • Try it
