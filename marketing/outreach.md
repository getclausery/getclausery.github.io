# Outreach emails

Send each email individually, to a named person where possible, and change the first line so it's clearly about their page. Send at most one follow-up, and never email someone again who has said no or asked not to be contacted. Ten personal emails beat a hundred templated ones.

Keep a simple sheet: date, site, page URL, contact, which email you sent, follow-up date, result. Replies usually come from 5–15% of good-fit pages. Each yes is a link that keeps sending visitors and helps search engines trust the domain.

## Finding the right pages

Search Google with queries like these, then pick pages that are maintained and actually list tools or templates:

- `"small business resources" "invoice template"`
- `intitle:"free invoice templates"` and `intitle:"invoice templates" freelancers`
- `"freelancer resources" OR "freelancing resources" site:.edu`
- `libguides "small business" templates` (public and university library guides)
- `SBDC "resources" invoice` and `SCORE chapter "templates"`
- `coworking "member resources" freelancers`
- `"tools for freelancers" 2026`

## 7. Asking your first customers for a review

```

Thanks for choosing Clausery Pro. It means a lot this early on.

If it's been useful, would you write a short, honest review on {G2 or Capterra link}? Two or three sentences about what you use it for helps other small businesses decide, and tells me what to improve.

{your name}
```

## 8. Learning from people who didn't buy

When someone emails support during or after their trial, ask:

```
Thanks for trying Pro! One question, if you have a moment: what would have made Pro worth paying for, or what stopped you? Even a one-line answer helps me a lot.
```
