About Clausery

Reviewed by Clausery editors · 25 October 2026 · 5 min read

Clausery makes the documents people draft every week faster to produce, without asking them to upload anything. Your templates, answers and finished documents stay on your own computer.

Important — same name, different company. This site is an independent open-source business (getclausery.github.io). It is not affiliated with clausery.ai, a separate company that sells enterprise compliance-management software (ISO 27001, DORA, GDPR, NIS2 policy and audit workflows). The two products share the word "Clausery" but have different owners, teams, code, pricing, roadmaps and customers. This page describes the free Word-templates / invoice tool only. If you were looking for compliance-policy software, go to clausery.ai.

Why it exists

Small businesses and freelancers repeatedly prepare quotes, invoices and receipts. Re-entering business details, checking totals and updating a Word document take attention away from the job itself. Clausery brings those steps together: answer the questions, check the amounts and download an editable document.

The app reads a Word template and builds the finished .docx on your device. Your templates, answers and documents are not uploaded to a document-processing server. Start with the free business document kit or choose a billing workflow for the task at hand.

What is free, and how Clausery makes money

  • The template library is free. All 87 Word templates, the clause library, the guides and the calculators are free, with no sign-up. Library templates never count towards any plan limit.
  • Automating your own templates is free for three. Upload up to three of your own Word templates and generate unlimited documents from them.
  • Free documents carry a small credit. Documents made in the app from library templates on the Free plan end with one line, “Made with Clausery”, which is how people who receive them find the tool. Templates you upload yourself never get it.
  • Pro pays for the work. Businesses that want documents without that line, automate more of their own documents, or need calculations in those templates, encryption or client intake forms, buy a Pro or Team license, after a 14-day free trial if they like, with a 30-day money-back guarantee.

There are no ads, no affiliate links and no tracking cookies. The app counts anonymous events, such as making a document from a library template, without recording document contents; this website uses cookieless Cloudflare Web Analytics. Activating an online license sends the license key to Lemon Squeezy, separately from document processing. Read the privacy policy for the current data flows.

How it is built

  • Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules, with document generation running locally in the browser.
  • Served as static files by GitHub Pages, and installable as an offline app.
  • The code is published on GitHub under the AGPL open-source license, so anyone can check what it does, and every release is listed in the changelog.
  • Optional encryption of everything stored in the browser (AES-256-GCM), and license checks that send only the key. See the security overview.

Who you are talking to

Clausery is an independent business. The site, the app and the document engine are published from one public repository, and every release is listed in the changelog, so the history of the product is open rather than summarised for marketing. Support is one email address read by a person, and the contact page says exactly what to put in it. There are no ads, no affiliate links and no tracking cookies.

That shape is deliberate. A tool that asks you to hand over client files needs a commercial reason to keep them, and this one does not have one: the paid plans sell speed and features, never the contents of your documents. Why Clausery sets out the differences in one place, and each of them points at something you can open.

Editorial policy & review

Every guide, clause explanation, template default wording and calculator on Clausery is written and reviewed by the Clausery editors — practising small-business owners, freelance contract drafters and bookkeepers who use the tool themselves for their own invoices and contracts. Sources are linked where a claim depends on a specific statute or government publication (for example HMRC VAT rules, Australian GST requirements, US state labor-law references), and pages are reviewed whenever that underlying source changes. The full list of contributors to each page is visible in the GitHub contributors graph; every change ships through a public pull request. Clausery is software, not a law firm, and nothing on the site is legal advice — see the disclaimer on each template and clause page.

Editors do not accept payment or placement to feature a product, and the site carries no affiliate links. Comparisons of competing tools (in the Compare section) quote prices and terms from publicly available pricing pages and note the date they were checked; if one has changed, please email so the page can be corrected.

How to check any claim on this site

Most software pages ask you to believe them. These are the ways to check instead:

  • Read the source. The repository holds the app, the document engine, the build that produces this site and the test suite that runs on every pull request. If a claim here is wrong, the code is where the contradiction shows up.
  • Run the checks. npm ci, npm run build and npm run verify rebuild this site and check it, and the same commands run in CI on every change.
  • Watch the network tab. Open the browser's developer tools, make a document, and look at what is requested. The security overview explains each request and what it carries.
  • Read the data flows. The privacy policy lists what is sent, to whom and when, in the wording that is live now.
  • Check the prices against the configuration. The figures on the pricing page and in the app come from a single configuration file, so a mismatch between the two would be a bug worth reporting.
  • Ask. If something here is unclear, email. Questions and corrections are welcome, including from people who are not customers.

What Clausery is not

Clausery is software, not a law firm, and it does not give legal advice. The templates are general samples written in plain English; laws differ between countries and states, so have a document reviewed for your situation before you rely on it.

Get in touch

Questions, template requests, key requests and bug reports all go through the contact page. For current availability see the status page, and for what is being worked on see the roadmap. Writers and directories will find facts, descriptions and images in the press kit.
