US at a glance#

CurrencyUS dollar ($ / USD)
Date formatMonth / day / year (e.g. November 7, 2026)
Sales taxNot shown — for services where you do not collect sales tax; add a line if you do
Payment termsCommonly Net 30 (due in 30 days); Net 15, due on receipt and 50% deposit up front also used
Late feesAllowed in most states if agreed up front in a contract; commonly 1.5% per month (18% APR)

What the invoice must show#

  • A unique invoice number.
  • Your business/trading name and contact details (address, email, phone).
  • The client’s name and billing address.
  • Invoice date and payment due date.
  • A description of the goods or services provided.
  • For each line: quantity, rate, and amount.
  • Subtotal, any applicable sales tax (if collected), and the total amount due in US dollars.
  • Payment terms and how to pay (ACH routing/account, check payable to, or online link).

More detail: How to Write an Invoice (2026): 7-Step Checklist.

How this template is set up#

  • No sales tax line by default (services are often not taxable; check your state rules).
  • Dates in month/day/year order.
  • Amounts in US dollars; two-decimal currency by default.
  • Optional late-fee note (e.g. 'A late fee of 1.5% per month applies to unpaid balances after 30 days').

US invoicing is largely contractual rather than prescribed by federal law. There is no federal requirement for a specific invoice format. Requirements vary by state and by whether you are selling goods or services. If you sell taxable goods or certain services, add a sales-tax line at your state and local rate. If you are VAT/GST registered in another country, use that country’s template instead.

Not registered? Most US freelancers and service-only small businesses do not need to register for or show sales tax on an invoice. If you sell taxable goods, or services in a state that taxes them, register for a sales tax permit in that state and add a tax line at the combined state + local rate.

Questions

Do I need an EIN on my invoice?

No. You can show your EIN (Employer Identification Number) for a business, but it is not legally required on an invoice. Sole proprietors often use their SSN, but for privacy you may prefer to give that only on a W-9, not the invoice itself.

What payment terms should I set?

Net 30 (payment due 30 days from the invoice date) is common for US business-to-business invoices. For new clients or larger projects, ask for a 50% deposit up front.

Can I charge late fees?

Yes, if the client agreed to them in a signed contract before you started work. A common rate is 1–1.5% per month on the unpaid balance. Some states cap late-fee rates, so check your state’s usury limits.

Do I have to charge sales tax on services?

Many US services are not subject to sales tax, but rules differ by state and some (e.g. SaaS, digital goods, certain repairs) are taxable in certain states. Check your state department of revenue.

Is there a federal invoice format I must follow?

No. US federal law does not prescribe an invoice format for private businesses, unlike VAT countries. An invoice just needs enough detail to evidence the debt and what was sold.
