Self-employed invoice template

Reviewed by Clausery editors · 7 November 2026 · 3 min read

A clean invoice designed for sole traders, freelancers and self-employed people: your personal or trading name, the client, clear line items for hours or project work, your VAT/GST number if you are registered, and simple payment terms. Fill it in online, have the totals calculated for you, and download as Word or PDF.

No sign-up. Editable Word (.docx) or print to PDF. Your answers stay in your browser. Updated 7 November 2026.

Self-employed invoices only need a handful of extra pieces of information beyond a standard invoice: your legal name (or trading-as name), whether you are VAT/GST registered, and clear payment terms that leave no doubt when you expect to be paid. This template includes all of them out of the box.

If you are a sole trader in the UK, add your name and address (and VAT number if registered). If you are a freelancer in the US, add your name and (if you use one) your business name, plus any sales-tax requirements. The template adjusts to either — simply leave tax rate at 0 if you are not registered.

What to include#

  • Your name and trading identity. Your legal name, plus "trading as [name]" if you use one. Sole traders in the UK must show their own name on invoices even if they trade under a business name.
  • Optional VAT or GST number. Add your VAT, GST/HST, ABN or sales-tax number if you are registered; leave blank if not.
  • Invoice number and dates. A unique invoice number, invoice date and due date. The next number in your sequence is suggested automatically.
  • Client details. Your client's name and billing address. Recent clients are saved for next time.
  • Line items. Description of work, quantity (hours, days or units), rate and amount. Works for hourly billing or fixed-fee projects.
  • Totals, tax and how to pay. Subtotal, any discount, tax, grand total and payment methods / bank details.

How to fill it in#

  1. Step 1: Open the self-employed invoice

    Click "Make a self-employed invoice" below. Your name and details are pre-filled if you have used Clausery before.

  2. Step 2: Add your client

    Pick a recent client or add their name and billing address.

  3. Step 3: Add the line items

    Enter each piece of work or expense with a description, quantity (use hours for hourly work) and rate.

  4. Step 4: Set tax if you are registered

    If you are VAT/GST registered set the correct rate; if not, leave tax at 0.

  5. Step 5: Download and send

    Download as Word to tweak the wording or as PDF to email straight to your client.

Questions

Do I need a limited company to invoice?

No. You can invoice as a sole trader, freelancer or self-employed person under your own name (or a trading-as name). The template works either way.

Do I need to put my address on the invoice?

In the UK, sole-trader invoices must show your own name and an address where documents can be delivered (even if you use a business name). In other countries a contact address is strongly recommended.

Do I add VAT if I am not registered?

No. Leave the tax rate at 0 if you are below the VAT/GST threshold. Registering for VAT is optional below the threshold; once registered you must add VAT to every invoice.

Can I add my logo?

Yes. Upload a logo before downloading; it appears in the header of the finished Word or PDF file.

Can I invoice hourly and fixed-fee work on the same invoice?

Yes. Use hours as the quantity with your hourly rate for hourly work, and quantity 1 with the fixed fee for project work. Each line totals separately.
