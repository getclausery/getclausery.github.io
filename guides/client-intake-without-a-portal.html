Client intake without a portal: collect answers by email, privately

Published 26 September 2026 · Updated 17 October 2026 · Automating Word templates

Client portals mean another login for the client and another system holding their data. Clausery takes a different route: the questionnaire travels as a file.

How it works

  1. In any draft, open the review step and choose Create client intake form. You get a single HTML file that contains only the questions, with no document and no previous answers.
  2. Email it to the client, or share it the way you already share documents with them.
  3. The client double-clicks the file. It opens in their browser, works offline, validates their answers and lets them save progress.
  4. When finished they save an answers file and send it back. You import it into the draft and generate the document.

Why firms like it

  • No accounts, passwords or portal invitations for clients.
  • The form cannot send data anywhere; its security policy blocks all network access.
  • Answers arrive as one tidy file instead of an email thread.

What to ask

A good intake form collects only what the document actually needs. Start with the pieces every matter shares:

  • Who the client is. Full legal name, trading name, address, registration or tax number, and the person who will sign.
  • Who is on the other side. The other party's legal name and address, and whether they are an individual or a company.
  • The matter. A plain description of what the client wants, the documents they already have, and any deadline.
  • Money. Fees, budget, payment terms and who is paying.
  • Conflicts and consent. Whether the client has worked with the other side, and any consent or disclosure your rules require.

Leave out identity documents, bank passwords, medical records and anything else you do not need for the first draft. If you need an identity check, collect it through a separate process with its own retention rule.

Structure the form around the document

Group questions the way the document is structured: parties, term, scope, money, special terms. Use a section for each, with help text that explains why a question is asked. Make optional questions genuinely optional; a form that demands a value for every field produces invented answers.

Conditional questions keep the form short. If the client is a company, ask for the company number and director; if they are an individual, skip those. Clausery's designer lets a question depend on an earlier answer, so the client never sees a field that does not apply.

Test the form before a client sees it

  1. Fill it in as a company, then as an individual, and check that the right questions appear.
  2. Save partway through, close the file and reopen it, to confirm progress is kept.
  3. Return the answers file to yourself and import it into a draft.
  4. Generate the document and read the output, not just the answers.

When a portal is the better answer

An offline form is ideal for a one-off matter or a client who does not want another login. A portal earns its keep when several people collaborate on the same matter, when documents need to be reviewed and approved in one place, or when your regulator requires a particular audit trail. The point is to choose deliberately rather than defaulting to a portal because intake software exists.

Questions

Is an emailed intake form secure?

It is as secure as the email account and device at each end. The form itself contains no client data until the client fills it in, blocks network access, and is returned as a file the firm imports. For highly sensitive matters, use encrypted email or your usual secure file transfer.

Can clients fill in the form on a phone?

Yes. The form is a single HTML file that opens in a mobile browser and adjusts to the screen. Long forms are easier on a computer, so tell clients they can save progress and finish later.

What happens to the answers after the document is generated?

They stay in the browser workspace until the draft is deleted, and can be exported as JSON. Nothing is uploaded to Clausery. Set a retention rule in your practice for the downloaded files as well.
